Free Response Question 2 (12 Points)

Summary Statistics & Boxplot

An agricultural supervisor sampled \(N = 15\) organic Hass avocados from a commercial farm harvest and weighed each avocado in grams. The ordered data values are:

110, 125, 130, 132, 135, 138, 140, 142, 145, 148, 150, 155, 160, 165, 195

(a) Calculate the Five-Number Summary for this distribution (Minimum, Q1, Median, Q3, Maximum).

(b) Use the \(1.5 \times \text{IQR}\) rule to justify numerically whether any outliers exist in this dataset.

(a) Sketch a modified boxplot representing this distribution.

Scoring Guide & Solution

Part (a) Five-Number Summary Calculation:

  • Minimum: 110 grams
  • Median (\(Q_2\)): 8th ordered position = 142 grams
  • \(Q_1\): Median of lower 7 values (110, 125, 130, 132, 135, 138, 140) = 4th value = 132 grams
  • \(Q_3\): Median of upper 7 values (145, 148, 150, 155, 160, 165, 195) = 12th value = 155 grams
  • Maximum: 195 grams

Part (b) \(1.5 \times \text{IQR}\) Outlier Test:

\(\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1 = 155 - 132 = 23\text{ grams}\)

\(1.5 \times \text{IQR} = 1.5 \times 23 = 34.5\)

\(\text{Lower Cutoff} = Q_1 - 34.5 = 132 - 34.5 = 97.5\text{ grams}\)

\(\text{Upper Cutoff} = Q_3 + 34.5 = 155 + 34.5 = 189.5\text{ grams}\)

Since 195 > 189.5 grams, the value 195 grams is an extreme upper outlier. No values fall below 97.5 grams.

Free Response Question 3 (13 Points)

Z-Scores & Relative Standing

A university librarian analyzes the checkout duration (in days) for printed book loans across two regional library branches.

Branch A Summary Statistics: Mean = 14.2 days, Standard Deviation = 8.5 days, Minimum = 1 day, Maximum = 60 days.

Branch B Summary Statistics: Mean = 21.0 days, Standard Deviation = 5.0 days.

(a) Describe the shape of the distribution of book checkout durations for Branch A. Justify your answer based on the relationship between the mean, standard deviation, minimum, and maximum.

(b) A borrower kept a book from Branch A for 35 days. Calculate and interpret the \(z\)-score associated with this loan duration in context.

(c) Another student borrowed a book from Branch B and kept it for 31 days. Relative to their respective library system distributions, which student kept their book unusually longer? Justify using calculated \(z\)-scores.

Scoring Guide & Solution

Part (a) Shape Justification:

The distribution for Branch A is strongly right-skewed.
Justification: The minimum duration (1 day) is only \(\frac{14.2 - 1}{8.5} \approx 1.55\) standard deviations below the mean, whereas the maximum duration (60 days) is \(\frac{60 - 14.2}{8.5} \approx 5.39\) standard deviations above the mean. The fact that the upper extreme extends far more standard deviations above the mean than the lower extreme indicates a long tail to the right.

Part (b) Z-Score Calculation & Interpretation:

\(z = \frac{x - \bar{x}}{s} = \frac{35 - 14.2}{8.5} = \frac{20.8}{8.5} \approx +2.45\)

Interpretation: A checkout duration of 35 days at Branch A is 2.45 standard deviations above the mean loan duration for Branch A.

Part (c) Comparative Relative Standing:

Branch A Student Z-Score: \(z_A = +2.45\)

Branch B Student Z-Score: \(z_B = \frac{31 - 21.0}{5.0} = \frac{10.0}{5.0} = +2.00\)

Conclusion: The student at Branch A kept their book unusually longer relative to their branch distribution.
Justification: The \(z\)-score for the Branch A loan (\(z_A = 2.45\)) is greater than the \(z\)-score for the Branch B loan (\(z_B = 2.00\)), showing that 35 days is further out in the upper tail of Branch A's distribution than 31 days is for Branch B.

AP Statistics Unit 1 Formula Reference & Concepts

1. Summary Measures

Sample Mean: \(\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x_i}{n}\)

Sample Standard Deviation: \(s_x = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n - 1}}\)

Interquartile Range (IQR): \(\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1\)

2. Outlier Identification Rules

\(1.5 \times \text{IQR}\) Rule: Outliers exist if values are:

  • \(<\ Q_1 - 1.5(\text{IQR})\)
  • \(>\ Q_3 + 1.5(\text{IQR})\)

3. Standardization & Z-Scores

Z-Score Formula: \(z = \frac{x - \text{mean}}{\text{standard deviation}}\)

Standardizing transforms mean to 0 and standard deviation to 1.

4. Resistant vs Non-Resistant Stats

Resistant to Outliers: Median, IQR

Non-Resistant (Sensitive to Skew): Mean, Standard Deviation, Range
